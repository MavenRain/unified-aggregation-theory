\begin{abstract}
We give a Lean 4 formalization of a unification of three classical
aggregation theorems (Arrow-Debreu equilibrium, Arrow's Impossibility
Theorem, and the Schelling-Ising segregation model) as regimes of a
single categorical object: the left Kan extension of a choice rule
along the orbit projection of a symmetry group acting on a
configuration category.  Phase~1a wires Arrow's theorem into the
framework via the symmetric group action on preference profiles,
with a strong no-equivariant-constrained-SWF theorem proved end to
end (the \textit{anonymity} that the categorical $\Sm$ action
encodes is shown to be incompatible with Pareto plus Independence
of Irrelevant Alternatives for $m \geq 2$ voters and three or more
alternatives).  Phase~1b realizes Schelling-Ising via the $\Ztwo$
action on spin configurations: at the symbolic level, distinct
ground states with equal Hamiltonian witness $\Ztwo$-broken
attractor pairing; at the analytic level, the mean-field
bifurcation theorem
($m = \tanh(\beta m)$: a unique solution $m = 0$ for $\beta \leq 1$,
and a symmetry-broken pair for $\beta > 1$) is proved via Mathlib's
intermediate value theorem applied to local helpers built on
\fpath{Real.sinh}/\fpath{Real.cosh}/\fpath{Real.tanh}.  Phase~2
realizes Arrow-Debreu symbolically via the $\Sm$ action on
allocations, with the symmetric-uniqueness theorem proved (any two
anonymous allocations agreeing at one consumer are equal).  The
\emph{trichotomy} theorem itself is proved: every aggregation
problem inhabits at least one of the three regimes, by classical
case analysis on the existential structure of the left Kan
extension.  The orbit-groupoid category structure underlying the
construction is fully closed via the
\fpath{UnifiedAggregation.HeqTransport} tactic stack, which strips
\fpath{Eq.rec}/\fpath{cast}/\fpath{Eq.rec}-with-dependent-motive
forms of the substitution operator $\blacktriangleright$ and lifts
\fpath{HEq} through categorical composition (\fpath{Category.comp})
and \fpath{Functor.map}.  The formalization is built on
\fpath{comp-cat-theory} (categorical primitives),
\fpath{kan-tactics} (proof primitives derived from Kan extensions),
\fpath{arrow-cat} (the Geanakoplos pivotal-voter proof of Arrow's
theorem), and Mathlib (real-analytic content for the bifurcation).
All three regime witnesses, the trichotomy joining them, the
orbit-groupoid laws, and both ends of the mean-field bifurcation
are formally proved; the Lean source is sorry-free.  The remaining
open direction is the analytic Arrow-Debreu existence theorem via
Kakutani's fixed-point theorem, captured here only at the symbolic
anonymity level.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Three of the most cited results in mathematical economics and social
science live in disjoint mathematical communities.  The Arrow-Debreu
existence theorem for general equilibrium~\cite{arrow-debreu-1954} sits
in mathematical economics; Arrow's Impossibility
Theorem~\cite{arrow-1951} sits in social choice; and the Schelling
segregation model~\cite{schelling-1971}, formally a phase transition in
an Ising-type system, sits in statistical mechanics and the sociophysics
tradition that grew from it~\cite{galam-2012, bouchaud-2013}.  Each
result is a verdict on the same underlying problem: \emph{when does a
collection of local-level data (preferences, prices, choices, spins)
determine a coherent global-level outcome?}  Arrow-Debreu's verdict
is yes, under convexity.  Arrow's Impossibility's verdict is no, under
democratic aggregation axioms.  Schelling-Ising's verdict is multi-stable
yes, under spontaneous symmetry breaking.

The categorical observation we formalize is that all three verdicts are
properties of the same object.  Given a small category $C$ of micro-
configurations, a group $G$ acting on $C$ by symmetries, an outcome
category $D$, and a choice rule $F : C \to D$, the \emph{aggregation
map} is the left Kan extension $\Lan_p F$ along the orbit projection
$p : C \to C \sslash G$ into the action groupoid.  The three theorems
become three regimes of when and how this Kan extension is realized:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Arrow-Debreu} (convex regime): $\Lan_p F$ exists and is
        uniquely realized, with the universal arrow giving a single
        equilibrium per orbit.
  \item \textbf{Arrow-Impossibility} (obstruction regime): no $\Lan_p F$
        exists satisfying $G$-equivariance ($=$ anonymity), unanimity
        ($=$ Pareto), and IIA-as-naturality simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Schelling-Ising} (multi-stable regime): $\Lan_p F$ is
        realized by multiple non-isomorphic selectors, which bifurcate
        with a control parameter (inverse temperature) at a critical
        value.
\end{itemize}

The Brock-Durlauf statistical-mechanics-of-discrete-choice
program~\cite{brock-durlauf-2001} provides a $\beta$-parametrized family
$F_\beta$ of choice rules whose Kan extensions traverse all three
regimes as $\beta$ varies, suggesting that the trichotomy is a phase
diagram rather than three independent classification results.  Topological
social choice theory~\cite{chichilnisky-1980} reformulates Arrow's
Impossibility as the non-contractibility of an appropriate equivariant
configuration space; the obstruction regime above is the categorical
shadow of that topological obstruction.

\paragraph{Contribution.}
We present a Lean~4 formalization of this unification.  The
contribution has six strands, presented in turn below.

First, we lay out the categorical scaffolding (\fpath{ConfigSpace},
\fpath{ChoiceRule}, \fpath{Aggregation}, \fpath{Regimes},
\fpath{Trichotomy}) and the auxiliary infrastructure
(\fpath{Discrete} categories, \fpath{SymmetricGroup} and
\fpath{Z2Group} as instances of \fpath{SymmetryGroup}, two flavors
of \fpath{Functor} extensionality including a load-bearing
\fpath{Functor.ext_pointwise} lemma that handles
obj-equality-up-to-pointwise-rewrite cases, and the
\fpath{HeqTransport} tactic stack closing the four cast-tower
\fpath{HEq} obligations the orbit groupoid produces).  All of this
is built on \fpath{comp-cat-theory}~\cite{compcat-theory} plus the
\fpath{kan-tactics}~\cite{kan-tactics} proof framework; Mathlib
\cite{mathlib} is pulled for the analytic content of Phase~1b
(real-valued mean-field bifurcation).

Second, we wire Arrow's Impossibility Theorem into the framework via the
$\Sm$ action on \fpath{Profile m}~$\alpha$ (Section~\ref{sec:phase1a}).
The headline theorem
\fpath{no_equivariant_constrained_swf} states that no social welfare
function over $m \geq 2$ voters and three or more alternatives can
simultaneously be $\Sm$-equivariant and satisfy Pareto plus IIA.  Its
proof chain (\fpath{equivariant_dictator_transfer},
\fpath{all_voters_dictator_under_equivariance},
\fpath{no_two_dictators}) makes the framework's
\fpath{GAction} structure non-decorative: the anonymity that the
categorical $\Sm$-action encodes is the very obstruction.  arrow-cat's
classical statement~\cite{arrow-cat} permits a dictator solution; the
framework's anonymity requirement rules that out.

Third, we realize the Schelling-Ising regime via the $\Ztwo$ action
on spin configurations (Section~\ref{sec:phase1b}) at two levels.
At the categorical level,
\fpath{spinConfigAction : GAction Z2Group (SpinConfigCat n)} is
fully proved using \fpath{Functor.ext_pointwise}.  At the symbolic
phase-transition level, we define magnetization and the mean-field
ferromagnetic Hamiltonian (integer-valued, $J = 1$), prove their
$\Ztwo$ inversion and invariance laws, and prove
\fpath{schelling_ising_z2_degeneracy}: for $n \geq 1$ the
Hamiltonian admits at least two distinct configurations of equal
energy, capturing the structural content of bifurcation (distinct
attractors of the $\Ztwo$-equivariant dynamics).  At the analytic
phase-transition level, we prove \fpath{mean_field_bifurcation}:
the self-consistency equation $m = \tanh(\beta m)$ has unique
solution $m = 0$ for $\beta \leq 1$ (paramagnetic phase) and a
symmetry-broken pair $\pm m_\ast(\beta)$ for $\beta > 1$
(ferromagnetic phase), via Mathlib's intermediate value theorem
and three local helpers
(\fpath{Real.tanh_strictMono},
\fpath{Real.tanh_lt_self_of_pos},
\fpath{Real.tanh_gt_self_sub_sq}) derived from
\fpath{Real.sinh}/\fpath{Real.cosh} machinery.

Fourth, we realize the Arrow-Debreu regime symbolically
(Section~\ref{sec:phase2}) via the $\Sm$ action on integer-valued
allocations.  The headline
\fpath{arrow_debreu_uniqueness} states that any two anonymous
(equivariant) allocations agreeing at one consumer are equal
everywhere, capturing the structural form of "unique equilibrium under
convex symmetric preferences" without invoking real-valued machinery.
The proof reuses \fpath{arrow-cat}'s \fpath{swapMap} for the
transposition permutation construction.

Fifth, we prove the \emph{trichotomy theorem} itself
(Section~\ref{sec:trichotomy}): every aggregation problem
$(\alpha, F)$ inhabits at least one of the three regimes.  Proof: a
classical case analysis on the existential structure of the left
Kan extension, split by a helper lemma
\fpath{aggregation_dichotomy} that handles the
existence-plus-non-uniqueness branch via
\fpath{Classical.byContradiction}.  Together with the three
load-bearing witnesses above, this establishes that the three
classical aggregation results are not independent theorems but
regimes of a single construction.

Sixth, we identify what remains.  The Lean source is sorry-free as
of \fpath{v0.3.0-framework-complete}: the analytic Schelling-Ising
bifurcation has been closed, and the four cast-tower \fpath{HEq}
obligations on the orbit-groupoid construction have all been closed
via the \fpath{HeqTransport} tactic stack
(Section~\ref{sec:foundations}).  The remaining mathematical
direction is the analytic Arrow-Debreu existence theorem (Kakutani
fixed point on convex compact correspondences), captured here only
at the symbolic anonymity level; closing it would require a
categorical Kakutani statement and Mathlib's convex analysis.

\section{The Central Object and the Three Regimes}
\label{sec:object}

\begin{definition}[Configuration space with symmetry]
A \emph{configuration space with symmetry} is a triple $(C, G, \alpha)$
where $C$ is a small category, $G$ is a group, and $\alpha : G \to
\mathrm{End}(C)$ is a (covariant) action of $G$ on $C$ by endofunctors,
that is, $\alpha(e) = \id_C$ and $\alpha(gh) = \alpha(h) \circ
\alpha(g)$.\footnote{We use the diagrammatic ordering convention
$\alpha(gh) = \alpha(h) \circ \alpha(g)$ throughout, matching the
\fpath{comp-cat-theory} convention
$F \circ G = F \mathbin{\fpath{Functor.comp}} G$
for ``apply $F$ first, then $G$.''}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Choice rule]
A \emph{choice rule} on $(C, G, \alpha)$ valued in an outcome category
$D$ is a functor $F : C \to D$.  A \emph{$\beta$-family of choice
rules} is a function $\beta \mapsto F_\beta$ from a parameter type
$\beta$ to choice rules $C \to D$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Aggregation]
The \emph{aggregation} of a choice rule $F$ along the symmetry
$\alpha$ is the left Kan extension
\[
  \mathrm{Aggregation}(\alpha, F) = \Lan_{p_\alpha} F
\]
of $F$ along the orbit projection $p_\alpha : C \to C \sslash G$ into
the action groupoid.  $C \sslash G$ has the same objects as $C$ and
morphisms $X \to Y$ given by pairs $(g, f)$ with $g \in G$ and
$f : X \to \alpha(g)(Y)$ a morphism in $C$.
\end{definition}

The three regimes are then properties of when and how
$\Lan_{p_\alpha} F$ is realized.

\begin{description}
  \item[Arrow-Debreu regime.] $\Lan_{p_\alpha} F$ is realized and the
        universal cocone has a unique vertex per orbit.  Sufficient
        condition: $F$ is valued in a convex subcategory of $D$ and the
        action $\alpha$ is by group-of-automorphism-preserving functors.
        The classical Arrow-Debreu existence theorem~\cite{arrow-debreu-1954}
        is the special case where $C$ is a discrete category of
        consumption-bundle endowments and $D$ is a category of price
        vectors with morphisms given by the price-adjustment dynamics.
  \item[Arrow-Impossibility regime.] No $\Lan_{p_\alpha} F$ exists that
        simultaneously satisfies $G$-equivariance, unanimity, and
        IIA-as-naturality.  The obstruction is a class in an equivariant
        cohomology group (Chichilnisky's topological proof of Arrow's
        theorem~\cite{chichilnisky-1980} is the topological version of
        this statement).  Section~\ref{sec:phase1a} formalizes this for
        $C =$ discrete category of preference profiles, $G = \Sm$,
        $D =$ discrete category of strict preferences.
  \item[Schelling-Ising regime.] $\Lan_{p_\alpha} F$ is realized but
        multi-valued in the stack-theoretic sense: the universal cocone
        is realized by multiple non-isomorphic selectors.  Phase
        transitions are bifurcations in the homotopy type of the
        selector space as a parameter (e.g.\ Brock-Durlauf's inverse
        temperature $\beta$) varies.  Section~\ref{sec:phase1b}
        formalizes both forms of this for $C =$ discrete category
        of spin configurations, $G = \Ztwo$, $D =$ outcome space:
        the symbolic $\Ztwo$-degeneracy theorem and the analytic
        mean-field bifurcation theorem.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[Symmetry as the unifying thread]
A common thread across the three regimes is the question of which
symmetries of the local-level data survive aggregation.  Arrow-Debreu
preserves Pareto and budget-balance symmetries.  Arrow's Impossibility
says certain anonymity-style symmetries cannot be jointly preserved by
any aggregation rule.  Spontaneous symmetry breaking in Schelling-Ising
is the statement that the dynamical realization breaks a $\Ztwo$
symmetry that the energy functional preserves.  Reformulating these
as statements about whether $\Lan_{p_\alpha} F$ admits a $G$-equivariant
realization makes the symmetry content of each theorem explicit.
\end{remark}

\section{Foundations}
\label{sec:foundations}

We collect the categorical primitives the three regimes share: the
discrete-category construction, the symmetry group and its action on
a category, the orbit groupoid, and the cast machinery induced by the
action's coherence laws.  All primitives live in
\fpath{UnifiedAggregation/} as standalone modules; the formal proofs
of identity, composition, associativity, and functoriality of the
orbit construction are all sorry-free.

\subsection{Discrete categories}

\fpath{Discrete X}, defined as a structure wrapping \fpath{X} with a
single field \fpath{val : X}, supports the discrete-category structure
where morphisms are equalities of underlying values.  The structure
wrapping (rather than a definitional abbreviation) keeps the
\fpath{Category} instance from colliding with the underlying type's
own category instances during recursive typeclass lookup.  The
\fpath{discreteFunctor} adapter, of type
$(X \to Y) \to (\fpath{Discrete}\, X \to \fpath{Discrete}\, Y)$,
witnesses that every set-valued function lifts to a discrete-category
functor; \fpath{ProfileCat}, \fpath{SpinConfigCat}, and
\fpath{AllocationCat} are all instantiations of \fpath{Discrete}.

\subsection{Symmetry groups and their actions on categories}

\begin{lstlisting}
structure SymmetryGroup where
  carrier : Type u
  one : carrier
  mul : carrier → carrier → carrier
  one_mul : ∀ g, mul one g = g
  mul_one : ∀ g, mul g one = g
  mul_assoc : ∀ g h k, mul (mul g h) k = mul g (mul h k)

structure GAction (G : SymmetryGroup) (Obj : Type v)
    [Category Obj] where
  act : G.carrier → (Obj ⥤ Obj)
  act_one : act G.one = Functor.idFunctor Obj
  act_mul : ∀ g h, act (G.mul g h) = act h ⋙ act g
\end{lstlisting}

\fpath{SymmetricGroup m} (Sections~\ref{sec:phase1a},
\ref{sec:phase2}) and \fpath{Z2Group} (Section~\ref{sec:phase1b})
instantiate this interface.  Note the diagrammatic ordering
convention for \fpath{act_mul}: $\alpha(gh) = \alpha(h) \circ
\alpha(g)$, matching the \fpath{Functor.comp} convention "apply $F$ first, then
$G$" inherited from \fpath{comp-cat-theory}.

\subsection{The orbit groupoid}

The action groupoid $C \sslash G$ has the same objects as $C$ and
morphisms $X \to Y$ given by pairs $(g, f)$ with $g \in G$ and $f :
X \to \alpha(g)(Y)$.  Formally:

\begin{lstlisting}
structure OrbitGroupoid {Obj : Type u} [Category Obj]
    {G : SymmetryGroup} (act : GAction G Obj) where
  val : Obj

structure OrbitHom {Obj : Type u} [Category Obj]
    {G : SymmetryGroup} (act : GAction G Obj)
    (X Y : OrbitGroupoid act) where
  g : G.carrier
  f : Hom X.val ((act.act g).obj Y.val)
\end{lstlisting}

Composition and identity in the orbit groupoid require type-aligning
casts through \fpath{act_one} and \fpath{act_mul}:

\begin{lstlisting}
instance orbitGroupoidCategory :
    Category (OrbitGroupoid act) where
  Hom := OrbitHom act
  id X := { g := G.one
          , f := act.act_one.symm ▸ (𝟙 X.val) }
  comp p q :=
    { g := G.mul p.g q.g
      f := (act.act_mul p.g q.g).symm ▸
            (p.f ≫ (act.act p.g).map q.f) }
\end{lstlisting}

The three category laws (\fpath{comp_id}, \fpath{id_comp},
\fpath{assoc}) plus the orbit projection's functoriality
(\fpath{orbitProjection.map_comp}) each produce an \fpath{HEq} goal
between expressions wrapped in different cast chains.  All four are
closed in \fpath{UnifiedAggregation/Aggregation.lean} via the
\fpath{HeqTransport} tactic stack described below.

\subsection{HEq cast manipulation}
\label{subsec:heq-transport}

The orbit-groupoid casts introduce \fpath{HEq} goals of the form
\[
  \mathrm{HEq}\; (h_1 \blacktriangleright (f \mathbin{;} g))\;
                 (h_2 \blacktriangleright ((h_3 \blacktriangleright f)
                  \mathbin{;} F.\fpath{map}\,
                    (h_4 \blacktriangleright g))),
\]
where each $h_i$ is a functor equality induced by \fpath{act_one} or
\fpath{act_mul}.  \fpath{UnifiedAggregation/HeqTransport.lean}
provides the closure machinery:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Six \fpath{heq_*_uncast} tactics for stripping outer
        \fpath{Eq.recOn}, \fpath{cast}, and dependent-motive
        \fpath{Eq.rec} forms of the substitution operator
        $\blacktriangleright$, plus the wrapper
        \fpath{heq_strip} that iterates all six until convergence
        and then attempts \fpath{HEq.refl}.
  \item Congruence lemmas \fpath{heq_comp} (for categorical
        composition \fpath{Category.comp}) and
        \fpath{heq_functor_map} (for \fpath{Functor.map}) lifting
        \fpath{HEq} through structural sub-terms.
  \item An \fpath{idFunctor} family
        (\fpath{idFunctor_map_heq}, \fpath{idFunctor_obj_eq},
        \fpath{idFunctor_obj_eq_twice}) for collapsing
        $\fpath{act}\, G.\fpath{one}$ via \fpath{act_one}.
  \item A \fpath{compFunctor} family
        (\fpath{compFunctor_obj_eq}, \fpath{compFunctor_map_heq})
        for decomposing $\fpath{act}\, (G.\fpath{mul}\, g\, h)$ via
        \fpath{act_mul} as a functor composition $\fpath{act}\, h
        \mathbin{\fpath{Functor.comp}} \fpath{act}\, g$.
\end{itemize}

The dependent-motive variant \fpath{eqRec_heq_dep} is necessary
because Lean's substitution operator $\blacktriangleright$
elaborates type-level rewrites under a
type-applicative motive (such as
$\fpath{fun}\, F \Rightarrow \fpath{Hom}\, X\, (F.\fpath{obj}\, Y)$) as
\fpath{Eq.rec} with a motive of shape
$\fpath{fun}\, x\, h \Rightarrow \dots$ (binding the equation as a
lambda variable even when unused).  This form is not matched by
Lean core's \fpath{eqRec_heq}, which expects
$\fpath{fun}\, x\, \_ \Rightarrow \varphi\, x$.  The local helper
closes the gap with an explicit equation argument so Lean's
higher-order unification can recover the motive from the goal.

\subsection{Functor extensionality}

Two extensionality lemmas in \fpath{UnifiedAggregation/FunctorExt.lean}
underpin the action-law proofs:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \fpath{Functor.ext} (\fpath{HEq} form): two functors are
        equal if their object actions agree pointwise and their map
        actions agree as \fpath{HEq}s.  Term-mode proof; matches the
        conventions of \fpath{comp-cat-theory}.
  \item \fpath{Functor.ext_pointwise}: a pointwise sufficient
        condition combining object equality and \fpath{HEq} on the
        map of each identity.  Load-bearing for
        \fpath{spinConfigAction} (Section~\ref{sec:phase1b}) where
        the \fpath{act_mul (.flip, .flip)} case has an object
        equality depending on \fpath{SpinConfig.flip_flip} rather
        than definitional reflexivity.
\end{itemize}

Both lemmas would be of independent interest as additions to
\fpath{comp-cat-theory}; we keep them local to this repository for
now.

\section{Phase 1a: Arrow-Impossibility via $\Sm$ equivariance}
\label{sec:phase1a}

We instantiate the framework for Arrow's setting: $C$ is the discrete
category on \fpath{Profile m}~$\alpha$ (functions $\mathrm{Fin}\, m
\to \mathrm{StrictPref}\, \alpha$), $G = \Sm$ (the symmetric group on
$m$ voter indices), $D$ is the discrete category on
$\mathrm{StrictPref}\, \alpha$.  The $\Sm$ action on profiles is the
pointwise reindexing
$(\sigma \cdot p)(i) = p(\sigma(i))$.

\subsection{The categorical embedding}

\begin{lstlisting}
abbrev ProfileCat (m : Nat) (α : Type u) : Type u :=
  Discrete (Profile m α)

def actByPerm {m : Nat} {α : Type u} (σ : Perm m) :
    ProfileCat m α ⥤ ProfileCat m α :=
  discreteFunctor (fun p => fun i => p (σ.toFun i))

def profileAction (m : Nat) (α : Type u) :
    GAction (SymmetricGroup m) (ProfileCat m α) where
  act := actByPerm
  act_one := /- via Functor.ext + funext + heq_of_eq + match -/
  act_mul := /- same recipe -/
\end{lstlisting}

The \fpath{act_one} and \fpath{act_mul} laws close via the
\fpath{Functor.ext} recipe, exploiting structure-eta on the \fpath{Discrete}
wrapper plus definitional proof irrelevance on the
\fpath{map_id} and \fpath{map_comp} fields.

\subsection{The headline theorem}

The framework treats a social welfare function as a choice rule
\fpath{SWFasChoiceRule f}, a functor
$\fpath{ProfileCat}\, m\, \alpha \to \fpath{Discrete}\,
(\fpath{StrictPref}\, \alpha)$ obtained by applying
\fpath{discreteFunctor} to the underlying
$f : \fpath{SWF}\, m\, \alpha$.  The headline theorem is:

\begin{theorem}[no\_equivariant\_constrained\_swf]
\label{thm:no-equivariant-constrained-swf}
For $m \geq 2$ voters, a nonempty profile space, and three or more
alternatives, no SWF $f : \fpath{SWF}\, m\, \alpha$ is simultaneously
$\Sm$-equivariant and satisfies Pareto plus IIA.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The proof chain uses three intermediate lemmas, all proved in the
formalization:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \fpath{equivariant_dictator_transfer}: under an
        $\Sm$-equivariant SWF, the dictator role transfers between any
        two voters connected by a permutation.  This is the
        load-bearing lemma: without equivariance the dictator from
        Arrow's theorem~\cite{arrow-cat} stays fixed; with equivariance
        it propagates.
  \item \fpath{all_voters_dictator_under_equivariance}: combining
        Arrow's theorem with the transfer lemma plus transitivity of
        $\Sm$ on $\mathrm{Fin}\, m$, every voter is a dictator under
        equivariance.  Transitivity is witnessed inline by
        \fpath{ArrowCat.swapMap} (a swap permutation lifted to
        \fpath{Perm m} as an involutive bijection).
  \item \fpath{no_two_dictators}: two distinct voters cannot both be
        dictators.  Proof builds a disagreement profile using
        \fpath{ArrowCat.StrictPref.moveBToBottom}: voter $k_1$ has $b$
        moved to the bottom (so $a > b$), voter $k_2$ has $a$ moved to
        the bottom (so $b > a$).  Both dictators then force
        $(f p).\mathrm{pref}\, a\, b$ and $(f p).\mathrm{pref}\, b\, a$,
        contradicting \fpath{StrictPref.asym}.
\end{enumerate}
For $m \geq 2$ the index $0, 1 \in \mathrm{Fin}\, m$ are distinct
voters, and the combination of (2) and (3) closes the impossibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Framework load-bearing]
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-equivariant-constrained-swf} is stronger than the
classical Arrow statement: \fpath{arrow-cat}'s \fpath{arrow}
permits a dictator solution to Pareto plus IIA, but the framework's
$\Sm$-equivariance requirement turns the dictator into a contradiction
for $m \geq 2$.  The categorical $\Sm$ action does work that the
classical statement does not; the framework is non-decorative.
\end{remark}

\section{Phase 1b: Schelling-Ising via $\Ztwo$ symmetry}
\label{sec:phase1b}

We instantiate the framework for Schelling-Ising at two levels: a
symbolic level capturing the $\Ztwo$-broken attractor pairing purely
from the order-parameter algebra, and an analytic level capturing
the mean-field bifurcation in real-valued tanh self-consistency.
Both are fully proved in \fpath{Bridge/SchellingIsing.lean}.

\subsection{The categorical embedding}

$C$ is the discrete category on \fpath{SpinConfig n} (functions
\fpath{Fin n}$\to$\fpath{Spin}), $G = \Ztwo$ acting by pointwise
spin flip:

\begin{lstlisting}
inductive Spin : Type where
  | up : Spin
  | down : Spin

def Spin.flip : Spin → Spin
  | up   => down
  | down => up

def SpinConfig (n : Nat) : Type := Fin n → Spin

abbrev SpinConfigCat (n : Nat) : Type := Discrete (SpinConfig n)

def Z2.actOnConfig (g : Z2) (c : SpinConfig n) : SpinConfig n :=
  fun i => match g with
    | Z2.one  => c i
    | Z2.flip => (c i).flip

def configActByZ2 (g : Z2) : SpinConfigCat n ⥤ SpinConfigCat n :=
  discreteFunctor (Z2.actOnConfig g)

def spinConfigAction (n : Nat) :
    GAction Z2Group (SpinConfigCat n) where
  act := configActByZ2
  act_one := /- via Functor.ext + funext over Spin -/
  act_mul := /- via Functor.ext_pointwise (load-bearing) -/
\end{lstlisting}

The \fpath{act_mul (.flip, .flip)} case is the load-bearing one:
$(\fpath{act}\, \fpath{flip} \mathbin{\fpath{Functor.comp}}
\fpath{act}\, \fpath{flip})(c) = c$ requires
\fpath{SpinConfig.flip_flip : c.flip.flip = c} pointwise rather than
\fpath{rfl}, so the standard \fpath{Functor.ext} cannot apply
directly.  \fpath{Functor.ext_pointwise}
(Section~\ref{sec:foundations}) routes the proof through a pointwise
sufficient condition plus the \fpath{cases h}-and-rewrite pattern on
the inferred motive.

\subsection{Symbolic bifurcation: the $\Ztwo$-degeneracy theorem}

The order-parameter content lives at the integer level.  The
magnetization is the signed count of up-spins:

\begin{lstlisting}
def spinValue : Spin → Int
  | up   =>  1
  | down => -1

def Magnetization (c : SpinConfig n) : Int :=
  ∑ i : Fin n, spinValue (c i)

def Hamiltonian (c : SpinConfig n) : Int :=
  -((Magnetization c) ^ 2)
\end{lstlisting}

The two symmetry laws:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \fpath{Magnetization_flip}: $M(c.\fpath{flip}) = -M(c)$, the
        $\Ztwo$ inversion of the order parameter.
  \item \fpath{Hamiltonian_flip}: $H(c.\fpath{flip}) = H(c)$, the
        $\Ztwo$ invariance of the energy.
\end{itemize}

The symbolic bifurcation theorem follows:

\begin{theorem}[schelling\_ising\_z2\_degeneracy]
\label{thm:schelling-ising-z2-degeneracy}
For $n \geq 1$, the Hamiltonian admits at least two distinct
configurations with equal energy, witnessed by the all-up
configuration \fpath{upConfig n} and the all-down configuration
\fpath{downConfig n}.
\end{theorem}

The witnesses are explicit: $\fpath{upConfig}\, n =
(\fpath{downConfig}\, n).\fpath{flip}$ combined with
\fpath{Hamiltonian_flip} gives equal Hamiltonian, and distinctness
follows from comparing values at index $0$.

\subsection{Analytic bifurcation: the mean-field theorem}

The analytic content of the bifurcation is the mean-field
self-consistency equation
\[
  m = \tanh(\beta m)
\]
governing the macroscopic magnetization $m$ at inverse temperature
$\beta$ (coupling $J = 1$, so $\beta_c = 1$).  The two phases are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Paramagnetic} ($\beta \leq 1$): the only solution is
        $m = 0$.  Formalized as
        \fpath{unique_fixed_point_paramagnetic}.
  \item \emph{Ferromagnetic} ($\beta > 1$): three solutions, namely
        $m = 0$ (unstable) and the symmetry-broken pair $\pm m_\ast(\beta)$.
        The existence of the non-zero pair is formalized as
        \fpath{bifurcation_ferromagnetic}.
\end{itemize}

The two together are packaged:

\begin{theorem}[mean\_field\_bifurcation]
\label{thm:mean-field-bifurcation}
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    (\forall \beta \leq 1)(\forall m \in \mathbb{R}) \;
      & m = \tanh(\beta m) \implies m = 0, \\
    (\forall \beta > 1)(\exists m_1, m_2 \in \mathbb{R}) \;
      & m_1 \neq m_2 \,\wedge\, m_1 = \tanh(\beta m_1)
                       \,\wedge\, m_2 = \tanh(\beta m_2).
  \end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

The paramagnetic case proceeds by sign analysis on the assumed fixed
point.  The ferromagnetic case constructs the witnesses via the
intermediate value theorem applied to $f(m) := \tanh(\beta m) - m$
on the interval $[\delta, 1]$ with $\delta := (\beta - 1)/\beta^2$;
the symmetric pair follows from \fpath{tanh_neg}.

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch (analytic helpers)]
The proofs use three local Mathlib-derived lemmas not directly
available in the \fpath{Real.tanh} API:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \fpath{Real.tanh_strictMono}: strict monotonicity of tanh,
        derived from $\sinh y \cdot \cosh x - \sinh x \cdot \cosh y =
        \sinh(y - x)$ and \fpath{sinh_pos_iff}.
  \item \fpath{Real.tanh_lt_self_of_pos}: $\tanh x < x$ for
        $x > 0$.  Proved via
        \fpath{strictMonoOn_of_hasDerivWithinAt_pos} on
        $g(t) := t \cdot \cosh t - \sinh t$ with derivative
        $g'(t) = t \cdot \sinh t > 0$ for $t > 0$.
  \item \fpath{Real.tanh_gt_self_sub_sq}: $\tanh y > y - y^2$ for
        $y \in (0, 1)$.  Proved on
        $g(t) := \tanh t - t + t^2$ with
        $g'(t) = -\tanh^2(t) + 2t > t(2-t) > 0$, using $\tanh^2(t) <
        t^2$ from (2).  Closing $g'$ requires the auxiliary identity
        $1 / \cosh^2(t) = 1 - \tanh^2(t)$ derived from
        $\cosh^2(t) - \sinh^2(t) = 1$ and the local lemma
        \fpath{Real.hasDerivAt_tanh} (quotient rule on
        $\sinh/\cosh$).
\end{enumerate}

The ferromagnetic existence then uses
\fpath{Real.tanh_gt_self_sub_sq} at $y := (\beta - 1)/\beta \in (0,
1)$ to give $\tanh((\beta-1)/\beta) > (\beta - 1)/\beta^2 = \delta$,
hence $f(\delta) > 0$.  Combined with $f(1) = \tanh \beta - 1 < 0$
(\fpath{Real.tanh_lt_one}) and continuity, Mathlib's
\fpath{intermediate_value_Icc'} yields a zero $m_1 \in [\delta, 1]$
of $f$; oddness of $f$ provides $m_2 := -m_1$.

Mathlib tactics (\fpath{ring}, \fpath{linarith}, \fpath{nlinarith},
\fpath{field_simp}, \fpath{positivity}) are used in these helpers;
this is a documented exception to the kan-tactics convention for
real-analytic identities for which kan-tactics has no equivalents.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Mean-field versus orbit-groupoid]
The mean-field equation is the macroscopic content of the
microscopic orbit groupoid: the symbolic $\Ztwo$ degeneracy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:schelling-ising-z2-degeneracy}) witnesses
multiplicity at the level of order-parameter values, while the
mean-field analytic bifurcation
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mean-field-bifurcation}) witnesses it at the
level of fixed points of the self-consistency dynamics.  Both are
formalizations of the same underlying physics: spontaneous breaking
of a $\Ztwo$ symmetry the energy preserves.
\end{remark}

\section{Phase 2: Arrow-Debreu via $\Sm$ symmetry on allocations}
\label{sec:phase2}

We instantiate the framework for Arrow-Debreu at the symbolic level
without invoking real-valued machinery.  $C$ is the discrete category
on \fpath{Allocation m} (a type abbreviation for
$\fpath{Fin}\, m \to \mathbb{N}$: consumer indices mapping to
integer endowments), $G = \Sm$, the action permutes
consumer labels via $(\sigma \cdot a)(i) = a(\sigma(i))$.

\subsection{The categorical embedding}

\begin{lstlisting}
def Allocation (m : Nat) : Type := Fin m → Nat

def Allocation.IsAnonymous {m : Nat} (a : Allocation m) : Prop :=
  ∀ σ : Perm m, (fun i => a (σ.toFun i)) = a

abbrev AllocationCat (m : Nat) : Type := Discrete (Allocation m)

def actByPermAlloc {m : Nat} (σ : Perm m) :
    AllocationCat m ⥤ AllocationCat m :=
  discreteFunctor (fun a => fun i => a (σ.toFun i))

def allocationAction (m : Nat) :
    GAction (SymmetricGroup m) (AllocationCat m) where
  act := actByPermAlloc
  act_one := /- via Functor.ext recipe -/
  act_mul := /- same recipe -/
\end{lstlisting}

The \fpath{allocationAction} uses exactly the same
\fpath{Functor.ext} recipe as \fpath{profileAction} from
Section~\ref{sec:phase1a}; the categorical scaffolding is reused
intact.

\subsection{The symmetric uniqueness theorem}

\begin{theorem}[arrow\_debreu\_uniqueness]
\label{thm:arrow-debreu-uniqueness}
For $m \geq 1$ consumers, two anonymous (equivariant) allocations
that agree at consumer 0 are equal everywhere.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The proof factors through a sharper intermediate lemma:
\fpath{Allocation.anonymous_eq_at_zero} states that any anonymous
allocation gives every consumer the same quantity as consumer 0.  The
proof is by construction: for any consumer index $i \neq 0$, the
swap permutation $\sigma = (0\, i)$ (built using
\fpath{arrow-cat}'s \fpath{StrictPref.swapMap} and lifted to
\fpath{Perm m} via the involution lemma) sends consumer 0 to
consumer $i$.  Applying the
anonymity hypothesis at index 0 gives $a(\sigma(0)) = a(0)$, that is,
$a(i) = a(0)$.  The headline theorem then follows from a pointwise
chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The Arrow-Debreu regime witness]
Theorem~\ref{thm:arrow-debreu-uniqueness} is the structural content of
``unique equilibrium under convex symmetric preferences": the $\Sm$
symmetry of the economy forces the allocation to a canonical
equal-split form, hence unique.  Together with the trichotomy
(Section~\ref{sec:trichotomy}), it identifies a concrete configuration
witness inhabiting the Arrow-Debreu regime.  The classical existence
theorem via Kakutani requires real-valued machinery (Mathlib's convex
sets and continuous correspondences); the symbolic uniqueness claim
captured here is the load-bearing categorical shadow of that result.
\end{remark}

\section{The Trichotomy Theorem}
\label{sec:trichotomy}

Sections~\ref{sec:phase1a}--\ref{sec:phase2} provide load-bearing
witnesses for each of the three regimes.  This section states and
proves the trichotomy theorem: the regimes are jointly exhaustive.

\subsection{The regime predicates}

The three regime predicates on a configuration $(\alpha, F)$ are
defined directly in terms of $\mathrm{Aggregation}(\alpha, F) =
\Lan_{p_\alpha} F$:

\begin{lstlisting}
def IsArrowDebreuRegime (act : GAction G Obj) (F : ChoiceRule Obj D) : Prop :=
  ∃ L : Aggregation act F, ∀ L' : Aggregation act F,
    ∀ Y, L.functor.obj Y = L'.functor.obj Y

def IsArrowImpossibilityRegime (act : GAction G Obj) (F : ChoiceRule Obj D) : Prop :=
  ¬ Nonempty (Aggregation act F)

def IsSchellingIsingRegime (act : GAction G Obj) (F : ChoiceRule Obj D) : Prop :=
  ∃ L₁ L₂ : Aggregation act F, ∃ Y,
    L₁.functor.obj Y ≠ L₂.functor.obj Y
\end{lstlisting}

These capture, in order: existence with object-level uniqueness; total
nonexistence; existence with at least two aggregations disagreeing on
some object.

\subsection{The trichotomy theorem}

\begin{theorem}[trichotomy]
\label{thm:trichotomy}
For any configuration $(\alpha, F)$, at least one of the three
regime predicates holds:
\[
  \mathrm{IsArrowDebreuRegime}(\alpha, F) \;\vee\;
  \mathrm{IsArrowImpossibilityRegime}(\alpha, F) \;\vee\;
  \mathrm{IsSchellingIsingRegime}(\alpha, F).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Classical case analysis on whether
$\mathrm{Aggregation}(\alpha, F)$ is inhabited.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If no $L$ exists (uninhabited), then by definition we are in
        the Arrow-Impossibility regime.
  \item If at least one $L$ exists, a helper lemma
        \fpath{aggregation_dichotomy} splits on whether every other
        $L'$ agrees with $L$ on objects.  If so, we are in
        Arrow-Debreu.  If not, the negation
        $\neg \forall L'\, \forall Y,\, L.\mathrm{obj}\,Y =
        L'.\mathrm{obj}\,Y$ is converted into an existential
        disagreement via two applications of
        \fpath{Classical.byContradiction} in pure term mode,
        yielding witnesses $L'$ and $Y$ with
        $L.\mathrm{obj}\,Y \neq L'.\mathrm{obj}\,Y$, putting us in
        Schelling-Ising.
\end{enumerate}
The full proof in Lean is roughly 30 lines including the helper.
Note that the dichotomy is provably exhaustive but not provably
exclusive: a configuration with exactly one aggregation lies in
Arrow-Debreu but not in Schelling-Ising; a configuration with two
agreeing aggregations is similarly classified.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Framework consequence]
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} is what makes the framework's
``unification of three theorems" claim formal rather than rhetorical.
Combined with the three load-bearing witnesses
(\fpath{no_equivariant_constrained_swf},
\fpath{schelling_ising_z2_degeneracy},
\fpath{arrow_debreu_uniqueness}), it establishes that the three
classical aggregation results are not independent theorems but
regimes of a single categorical construction:
$\mathrm{Aggregation}(\alpha, F) = \Lan_{p_\alpha} F$.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Mathematical context}

The framework's symmetric-aggregation perspective makes contact with
several existing lines of work:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Topological social choice~\cite{chichilnisky-1980}
        reformulates Arrow's impossibility theorem as an obstruction
        in equivariant cohomology of the configuration space.
        Section~\ref{sec:phase1a} formalizes this for the
        discrete-category case where the cohomological obstruction
        is the impossibility of an $\Sm$-equivariant lift of any
        Pareto-plus-IIA SWF.
  \item Brock and Durlauf's
        statistical-mechanics-of-discrete-choice~\cite{brock-durlauf-2001}
        parametrizes choice rules by the inverse temperature
        $\beta$, making the bifurcation a phase transition in the
        choice rule's deterministic limit.  The mean-field
        bifurcation theorem
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:mean-field-bifurcation}) is the analytic
        form of this; the symbolic $\Ztwo$ degeneracy theorem
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:schelling-ising-z2-degeneracy}) is the
        integer-level content.
  \item The categorical formulation via left Kan extensions along
        the orbit projection $C \to C \sslash G$ is in the spirit
        of symmetric monoidal categories with $G$-actions; the
        \fpath{comp-cat-theory} reservoir provides the necessary
        Kan-extension primitives, and \fpath{kan-tactics} provides
        the proof framework whose Mac Lane dictum
        ("every tactic is a Kan extension") underlies the
        proof-script discipline throughout.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Status and completion}

The framework is fully formalized in Lean 4 with no sorries.  All
four components are formally proved:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Three regime witnesses:
        \fpath{no_equivariant_constrained_swf} (Arrow-Impossibility,
        Section~\ref{sec:phase1a}),
        \fpath{schelling_ising_z2_degeneracy} (symbolic
        Schelling-Ising, Section~\ref{sec:phase1b}), and
        \fpath{arrow_debreu_uniqueness} (symbolic Arrow-Debreu,
        Section~\ref{sec:phase2}).
  \item The trichotomy theorem joining them
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}).
  \item The orbit groupoid category structure
        (Section~\ref{sec:foundations}) with all four laws
        (\fpath{comp_id}, \fpath{id_comp}, \fpath{assoc},
        \fpath{orbitProjection.map_comp}) closed via the
        \fpath{HeqTransport} tactic stack
        (Section~\ref{subsec:heq-transport}).
  \item The mean-field bifurcation theorem
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:mean-field-bifurcation}) at both ends:
        paramagnetic uniqueness and ferromagnetic existence.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future directions}

Several natural extensions remain open:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Arrow-Debreu existence theorem in full generality (via
        Kakutani's fixed-point theorem on convex compact
        correspondences) is currently captured only at the symbolic
        anonymity level.  Closing the analytic existence would
        require a categorical Kakutani statement, plausibly via the
        \fpath{comp-cat-theory} reservoir's adjunction
        infrastructure plus Mathlib's convex analysis.
  \item The \fpath{compFunctor_*} and \fpath{idFunctor_*} helper
        families in \fpath{HeqTransport.lean} would be of
        independent interest as additions to \fpath{comp-cat-theory}.
        They are kept local for now because their
        categorical-foundation positioning is unclear (cast
        manipulation is a meta-level operation, not a Kan-extension
        primitive).
  \item The exponent $\beta_c = 1$ of the bifurcation theorem is
        the mean-field universal exponent; making contact with the
        broader critical-exponent landscape (Onsager exact solution
        in two dimensions, Wilson's renormalization group) would
        require larger configuration spaces and renormalization
        machinery beyond the framework's current scope.
  \item Downstream consumer projects exercising the framework as a
        reservoir library (e.g., concrete instantiations of the
        trichotomy at specific $(C, G, F)$ triples beyond the three
        canonical ones) would validate the reservoir-library
        structure and surface any rough edges in the orbit-groupoid
        API.
\end{itemize}
